<h4>
Trade Route Design
</h4>

I want to use some of the multi-level clustering algorithms I have been designing for TSP as the main game mechanic. The basic idea is that there are a set of operations that you can do over a set of points:


- Pipe between two points to create a knot
- Grow a Knot by one point
- Pipe between two knots to create a higher order knot
- Grow a Knot by one Knot (cutting through the knot that is joining)
- Collapse two linked paths of knots into one knot (cutting through all of the knots along the path, converting from double pipes to single pipes)

And from the basic operations you can build a hierarchy of importance between the various points and knots of the set (knot here meaning a grouping of points and knots). I think this will allow players to easily design trade routes if some of the steps are automated (e.g. assigning all points to a knot initially cuts out a lot of the tedium). Then the up/down dpad would be used to go up or down the hierarchy and the left/right d-pad would be used to go clockwise/anticlockwise around the selected knot


<h4>Economy Model</h4>

Prices and availability emerge from supply/demand and transport cost/time.
Tariffs/embargoes, piracy, and wars alter costs and volatility. 
Stockpiles buffer shocks; perishables decay. town traits set baseline 
endowments and governance (order/corruption) that shape friction and espionage difficulty.

Corporate Espionage and Diplomacy
</h4>

Most spying mechanics act as hidden resource gates or alternative resources with higher risk/reward.

I want it to be more like you spend money/effort spying and you learn about your competitors trading routes in bits and pieces

So if you have some overlapping piece of trading space in your networks and you successfully infiltrate the organization then you can see some portion of their trade routes. Then you use this information in a few ways:

Information model: partial route reveal (volumes, timings, hub identities, profit earned along a section),
noisy and time‑decaying. Counter‑intel raises detection risk and adds red
herrings. Espionage should create actionable levers (reroute your own routes, undercut their prices to make their route unprofitable, 
legislate to ban certain goods they are making money off of, raid their routes hire privateers), not just a numeric buff.

If their trade route is faster than yours you modify your own to match theirs (automatically?)
Now that you know your rival is operating along a certain route you can try and bribe politicians and the cities to enact tariffs on their goods and other unfair trade practices

If they are trading heavily in a certain good, lobby those cities to require licences or higher regulation barriers that you have the funds to pay for and your rival does not

Pay privateers to rob along one of the routes and ignore your ships

If there are pirates in the towns you are competing over, offer tribute in exotic products, better weapons than they can buy, etc. or put them in positions of power in your own organization (They have high corruption levels but are extremely effective negociators and smugglers)

--- Vampire Theme and Conception of the Game ---

I am finding that it is difficult to design the game around multiple resources and while in the modern world there is a universal resource that is needed everywhere that being oil/electriciy, there seems to be no similar resource in the early modern period. Maybe it could be argued that lighting oil/whale oil provided a similar resource but with that resource the problem would be to go and find whales, oil wells, etc. and bring them directly to market with cities being only sinks and remote areas of geological importance being sole sources, this makes the logisitcs easier and non-loopy, you'd be better off with a spiderweb network than a loop ( reminds me of how ships leave China full and return from America empty). I was thinking that spices could be the missing resource since historically that is what drove trade networks and the global expansion of the VoC and BEIC, but there is a multi-sided marketplace that gets very complex very quickly. 

So I think we want one resource that is both consumed and produced by cities, and I was thinking that an economy of Vampires would work with our protagonist being a blood merchant having set up an organization to extract blood from their home city and transport and sell it to vampires in other cities
